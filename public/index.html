Methodology and limitations

How every number on this page is produced. No machine-learning model and no AI service is used.

1. Sentiment scoring (lexicon-based)

Each review is lowercased and split into whole words. Every word found in the positive lexicon adds 1, every word in the negative lexicon subtracts 1: score = positive matches − negative matches. Score > 0 is positive, < 0 negative, = 0 neutral. Matching is whole-word only (“slowly” does not match “slow”) and repeated words count each time. This is a transparent word list, not a trained model. It does not understand negation (“not good” scores +1), sarcasm or intensity. Lexicon fingerprint: –.

2. Weekly and monthly aggregation

Weeks follow ISO 8601 (Monday start). A review belongs to the week and month of its own date. A period’s value is the mean score of its reviews. A period with no reviews is shown as a gap, never as zero. Overall figures use all reviews directly (review-weighted); they are never an average of period averages. Each period is compared with the nearest earlier period that has data.

3. Negative outlier detection (z-score)

Only periods with at least 8 reviews (weeks) or 20 reviews (months) are eligible, and at least 6 eligible periods are needed. The mean and standard deviation of eligible period averages form the baseline. A period is flagged when z = (average − mean) / std is at or below −1.5; it is labelled severe at z ≤ −2.0, otherwise moderate. Only unusually low sentiment is flagged. A flagged period means “unusually low compared with the rest of the data”. It is not proof of an outage or of any cause. Weakness: a very bad period also lifts the standard deviation, which can hide milder ones (“masking”).

4. Complaint analysis and explanations

Only negative reviews are analysed. Keyword groups assign each one to one or more categories (delivery, quality, support, pricing, website, returns); a negative review with no keyword match goes to “other”. Each category counts distinct reviews, and its percentage is reviews in the category ÷ negative reviews. With fewer than 5 negative reviews the dashboard says the evidence is insufficient. Categories named in the text must appear in at least 2 reviews (at most 3 are named). Explanations are filled-in templates; every number comes from the data. They describe what customers reported, an association, not a verified technical cause.

5. Four ideas that are easy to mix up
  • Lexicon sentiment analysis: counting known positive and negative words to score one review.
  • Statistical outlier detection: asking whether a whole period’s average is unusually low compared with the others.
  • Complaint categorisation: sorting negative reviews into topics by keywords.
  • Evidence-based explanation: a template that reports the category counts found, with caveats, and claims nothing beyond them.
6. Known limitations
  • No negation, sarcasm, slang or multi-language support; a few synthetic demo reviews exist to show this.
  • Reviews scoring exactly 0 (including mixed ones) are neutral and are not part of the complaint analysis.
  • Keyword categories are simple and can miss unusual wording; unmatched complaints appear as “other”.
  • Weeks at the start or end of the data may be partial and therefore have few reviews.
  • The deployed site shows a snapshot: re-run the Python pipeline to refresh it.
